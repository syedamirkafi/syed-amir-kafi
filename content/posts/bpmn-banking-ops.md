At Dhaka Bank Ltd. I was an intern in Operations & Reporting — but the internship was really a crash course in business analysis. The branch's operational documentation was fragmented, and process knowledge lived inside people's heads.

## The assignment

Standardize operational processes so that a newcomer could step in without a month of oral tradition. That meant:

- **Documenting workflows** using the Flexcube core banking system as the anchor for each process.
- **Gathering requirements** from the people who actually ran each operation — not the manager's idealized version.
- **Writing documentation for the reader**, with clear steps, owners, and decision points.

## The core business-analysis stack

This role is where the BA toolkit became muscle memory:

- **BPMN / process mapping** — the language for describing how work flows and where it breaks.
- **Requirements gathering** — interviews, observation, and structured questions to extract the real process.
- **KPI design** — defining the measures that tell you whether a process is actually working.
- **Root cause analysis** — pushing past the surface symptom to the structural fix.

## The takeaway

Banks run on process discipline. Working inside one showed me why documentation must be written for the reader, not the author — and why requirements that are extracted from the people doing the work are the only requirements worth trusting.

> Documentation must be written for the reader, not the author.

That's the foundation I stand on for every business analysis and operations role since.
