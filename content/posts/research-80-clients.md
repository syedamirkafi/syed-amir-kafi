Before Germany, I spent a year as a Research Analyst at Assignoholic Research & Consultancy, supporting **80+ university clients** across business and IT domains. The clients were remote, the topics were varied, and the deadlines were tight.

## The discipline

Every project followed the same skeleton, whether it was a marketing dissertation or an IT systems review:

1. **Frame the question** — before any research, the problem had to be stated precisely enough to be answerable.
2. **Build the framework** — a structured analytical approach mapped to the research objectives.
3. **Source and verify** — evidence gathered and cross-checked, no anecdotes.
4. **Translate to action** — findings turned into structured reports and recommendations stakeholders could use.

## Why this matters for business analysis

Research and business analysis share one muscle: **the ability to take an ambiguous question and return a structured, evidence-backed answer.** That's the core of requirements gathering, of root-cause analysis, of KPI design — it all starts with framing the problem honestly.

## The takeaway

Working across 80+ different clients forced speed and structure simultaneously. You learn which questions matter, which sources are trustworthy, and how to communicate findings so non-specialists can act on them.

> Clear writing is the last mile of analysis.

That discipline — framing, structuring, verifying, translating — is exactly what I bring to analytics and business analysis roles today.
