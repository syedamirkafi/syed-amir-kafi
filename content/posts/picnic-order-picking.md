As a working student in Operations & Process Support at Picnic, I sat between warehouse, logistics, and customer service. The job title says "support," but the real task was: **find where the process leaks time, and fix it.**

## The leak

Order picking followed a process that had grown organically — steps added over time, owned by different teams, and never documented end-to-end. Nobody had a single picture of how an order moved from receipt to dispatch.

## What I did

1. **Documented the actual process** — walked each step with the teams who did it, mapping the real flow in BPMN-style diagrams rather than relying on the official one.
2. **Flagged the gaps** — identified redundant handoffs and validation steps between warehouse and logistics that added no value.
3. **Proposed the fix** — a streamlined workflow that preserved the checks that mattered while removing the busywork.

## The result

The proposal was implemented, and the order picking process went from its original step count to **2 steps fewer** — a small number that compounds when it's multiplied across thousands of orders and every picker on shift.

## The reflection

The 2-step reduction wasn't a technical breakthrough. It was a **process-analysis breakthrough**: the discipline of documenting reality before changing it, and coordinating the people who owned each step.

> Process documentation saves time downstream — for everyone.

This is the pattern I now apply everywhere: map the process, find the friction, remove the steps that don't serve the outcome.
