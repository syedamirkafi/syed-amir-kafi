When I joined Robi Axiata as a Territory Officer, the standard playbook was simple: visit more retailers, push the product, hope the numbers move. I wanted to test a different hypothesis — that the territory's sales data, read correctly, could tell us *where* the money was being lost before a single extra visit was made.

## The problem

The territory had 17 team members covering a large geographic area. Sales patterns were tracked, but only in aggregate. No one had asked: *which routes underperform and why? Which retailers deserve more coverage? Where is demand clustering?*

## The method

I pulled daily, weekly, and monthly performance data and broke it down by route, retailer type, and product line:

- **Route segmentation** — ranked routes by revenue, growth, and coverage cost to find high-potential corridors.
- **Coverage analysis** — compared visit frequency against sales yield to spot where effort was misallocated.
- **Pattern detection** — identified seasonal and weekly demand cycles for promotion timing.

## The result

- **+18% territory sales** through data-driven distribution and route optimization.
- **Data-backed decisions for upper management** via Tableau dashboards tracking daily, weekly, and monthly performance.
- **Ranked 13th nationwide out of 200+ Territory Officers** — measured impact through analysis, not just effort.

## The reflection

The lesson that stuck: **data work is only as good as the decision it changes.** Dashboards that sit unopened are decoration. The 18% didn't come from the analysis alone — it came from using the analysis to reallocate people, routes, and promotions toward where the data pointed.

> Clean data enables confident decisions — and confident decisions move operational metrics.

This is the core of how I approach analytics: start with the operational question, then let the data generate the answer, then *act* on it.
