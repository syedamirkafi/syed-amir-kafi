Companies use AI to filter candidates. I decided to use AI to *choose* companies. My job search in Germany runs on **career-ops**, an open-source system that turns an AI coding CLI into a job-search command center.

## The pipeline

Every job posting I'm considering goes through a structured evaluation:

- **A–G evaluation** — role summary, CV match, level strategy, compensation research, personalization plan, interview prep, and a legitimacy check that flags scams and ghost jobs.
- **ATS-optimized CV generation** — a tailored PDF per role, with keywords mirrored from the description.
- **A single tracker** — every application, status, and next action in one place, with integrity checks.

## Why this is a data-analyst's move

The system is basically an analytics pipeline applied to my own career:

- **It's data-driven** — 700+ offers evaluated, ranked by fit score, not by email-anxiety.
- **It's honest about quality** — the system explicitly recommends *not* applying to anything scoring below 4.0/5. Your time is the scarce resource.
- **It produces a story bank** — every evaluation generates STAR+R interview stories I can reuse across applications.

## The results so far

Tens of job offers evaluated with tailored CVs — and, more importantly, a search that runs on **evidence and structure** instead of spray-and-pray volume. When a role scores high, I have a research-backed CV, a story bank, and a negotiation plan ready.

## The point

This isn't a gimmick. It's the same discipline I bring to any data role: **frame the question, gather evidence, score honestly, act on the result.** My job search is just another domain where I get to practice it.

> Applications get you in the queue; research gets you a conversation.

It's open source at [career-ops.org](https://career-ops.org).
